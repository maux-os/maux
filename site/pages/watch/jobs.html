Process control on a 6502. A long pipeline is killed with ^C and the shell is back at once; ps shows the same processes as before, so nothing is left behind. Then vi is suspended with ^Z, jobs names it, and fg puts it back with its text intact.

The gate that proves it

This film is made by site/demos/jobs.py. Run as assertions, the same file is {{gen:demogate}}, and it runs with the rest of the tests. It requires that ps lists exactly two more processes, children of the shell, once the pipeline is started; that the pipeline is in the foreground, with no prompt, after fg; that after ^C ps lists exactly the processes it listed at the start; that the shell reports the editor Stopped and jobs names it; that the buffer is back on the screen after fg; and that the text survived into the saved file. The checks themselves were tested: with sleep replaced by a copy of true the demo fails at "ps now lists two more processes"; with vi replaced by true it fails at "vi is up".
