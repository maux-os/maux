What to type first

  1. ls /bin — the commands, all of them on the disk.
  2. ps — the processes. The BANK column is the memory bank each one lives in: that is what a process is here.
  3. man man — the manual, paged on the alternate screen. q leaves it and the screen comes back.
  4. cd /usr/src, then ls — maux's own source and its makefile, on the disk you are running.
  5. chvt 2 — a second console with a shell of its own; chvt 1 comes back to this one, exactly as you left it.

^C interrupts, ^Z suspends, and ^D ends input.

The machine

An enhanced IIe with a RamWorks-compatible card of {{fact:min_ram_kb}} KB, a mouse card in slot 5 for the 60 Hz clock tick, a Mockingboard in slot 4, and the release disk on a SmartPort card in slot 7. The card size is the smallest that covers the kernel's free list of process banks. The swatches under the screen pick the phosphor before the machine boots, and your choice follows you to the films.

The emulator

Apple2TS, by Chris Torrence and its contributors, MIT licensed and on GitHub. It models the enhanced IIe, the RamWorks card, the mouse and Mockingboard cards and the SmartPort drive, and it supplies the Apple ROMs, which is why maux ships none. The frame above runs the machine alone, so the screen gets the whole box; the link under it opens Apple2TS in its own tab with all of its controls -- reset, speed, saved states, its own disk drives.

maux's own gates boot this disk in the emulator's core, headless, and check that a session reaches a prompt and that the processes land in the banks the kernel says they should.

The same disk, on your own machine

It runs in MAME, and on a real enhanced IIe with a RamWorks card and a SmartPort adapter: Get maux has the download and both recipes.
